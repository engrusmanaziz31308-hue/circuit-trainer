Two-Way Switch Wiring Explained

Controlling one light from two places

Two-way switching lets you turn one light on or off from two different places, such as at the door and at the bed. It is very common on staircases and in long rooms.

The main parts

The circuit uses two two-way switches. Each has three terminals: a common terminal and two travellers. The two switches are linked by two traveller wires.

How it works

Operating either switch changes the path of the traveller wires, which opens or closes the lamp circuit. Either switch can therefore change the state of the lamp, no matter what position the other switch is in.

Safety first: switch off the MCB or main switch before starting work, and use a voltage tester to confirm that the wires are dead.

Common mistakes

  • Connecting the common terminals incorrectly, so the light cannot be controlled from both places.
  • Breaking the neutral through a switch, which is generally considered unsafe.
  • Leaving loose connections, which can cause heat and short circuits.

In the app

The app includes a two-way switch part, so you can see the connections on screen. Test the design in the app before doing any real installation.

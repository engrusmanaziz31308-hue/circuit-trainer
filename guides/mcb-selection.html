How to Choose an MCB Rating

Practical guide for home wiring

A Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB) switches off a circuit when the current becomes too high, before the cable overheats. The MCB rating must always be chosen to protect the cable. It is not a size you pick freely to make a circuit "work".

The basic rule

The MCB rating should be lower than or equal to the safe current carrying capacity of the cable it protects. If the MCB is larger than the cable can handle, the cable can overheat without the breaker tripping.

Common sizes in homes

UseTypical copper cableTypical MCB
Lights and fan points1.5 mm²6 A or 10 A
Power sockets (general appliances)2.5 mm²16 A
Air conditioner or geyser4 to 6 mm²20 A to 32 A

These are typical values only. The final choice depends on the actual load on that circuit and your local wiring standards, such as PEC in Pakistan.

How to calculate the current

Find the current an appliance draws from its power rating:

Current (A) = Power (W) ÷ (Voltage × Power factor)

For example, a 2000 W heater on 230 V draws about 8.7 A (power factor 1). On a circuit that also feeds other appliances, add up the total load before choosing the MCB.

Do not oversize the MCB to stop nuisance tripping. Frequent tripping means the load is too high, or there is a short circuit or leakage. Find and fix the fault.

Types of MCB

A single-pole (1P) MCB protects the live wire of a light or socket circuit. A double-pole (2P) MCB disconnects both live and neutral. A triple-pole (3P) MCB is used for three-phase motors. An RCCB or RCBO adds earth-leakage protection and is recommended for bathrooms, kitchens and outdoor sockets.

Summary

Always check the MCB rating and the cable size together. The "Cable Size & MCB" calculator in the app lets you enter a load and cable length for a quick estimate, but a qualified electrician should confirm the final installation.

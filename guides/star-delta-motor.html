Star-Delta Starter for Three-Phase Motors

Starting a large motor gently

A large three-phase motor started Direct-On-Line (DOL) can draw roughly 5 to 7 times its full-load current at start-up. This causes the supply voltage to dip, puts stress on cables and contactors, and gives the machine a sudden jolt. A star-delta starter reduces this starting current.

Star versus delta

Each winding of a motor can be connected in one of two ways:

ConnectionVoltage across each windingStarting currentStarting torque
Star (Y)About 58% of line voltageAbout one-third of DOLAbout one-third of DOL
Delta (Δ)Equal to line voltageFull DOL currentFull torque

The motor starts in star, which keeps the current low. After the motor reaches close to its normal speed, a timer switches it to delta for normal running.

Main parts of the starter

A typical starter has three contactors (main, star and delta), a timer, an overload relay, and start and stop buttons. The star and delta contactors must never close at the same time, so they are interlocked.

Safety: there must be a short delay between star and delta to avoid a dangerous short circuit. Have the control wiring checked by a qualified electrician.

When to use it

This method is usually used for motors above about 5 HP. Always check the motor nameplate, because some motors are designed only for delta connection.
